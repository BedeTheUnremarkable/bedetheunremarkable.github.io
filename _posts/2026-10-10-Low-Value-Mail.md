Today is World Mental Health Day, as well as Fiji Day and the National Day of Taiwan, although don't take the acknowledgement of that as an endorsement of any kind in the Greater China situation. I genuinely don't know enough to have an opinion about it.

It is particularly interesting that today is World Mental Health Day, because given recent events in my personal life, I would expect my own mental health to be in a far worse state. But no, despite some rather spectacular professional disappointments, health problems and intense episodes of existential anxiety, I feel surprisingly okay. Maybe it's because I've stopped feeling quite so burdened by conventional expectations of professional success, maybe it's because God is good and I've recently been making an effort to have a fruitful prayer life, or maybe it's because having fewer expectations to live up to has given me the freedom to entertain some rather unrealistic ambitions. Aside from getting ordained in the Church, which isn't an unrealistic career path if I'm willing to forgo marriage, I might as well try doing something like art or see where graduate education takes me once I finish my English degree. If conventional employment is going to remain such an uphill battle for me, I might as well see whether I can make something of the things I'm actually good at, rather than spend the rest of my life lamenting my inability to put the fries in the bag.

On art as a career, I was ruminating on making a splash into the field or subculture or whatever it is by doing some sort of performance art while my formal writing education continues and I gain visual art skills through my own training. From my own cursory research, framing seems to matter a lot, and it has to follow a relatively structured form involving declared intent, a constraint, documentation, etc. because while this is going to sound incredibly self-assured or narcissistic, what's really the difference between Chris Burden's "Five Day Locker Piece" or "747" and my idea for something like "The Portrait of the Typical Male Youth," a performance depicting a young man in a state of drunken resignation? Well, aside from Burden's MFA program pre-framing his stuff as art, it mostly seems to be planning and tolerance of legal and personal risk.

Now I know I sound like I'm being dismissive here, like the autodidact that thinks the whole art establishment is hackeneyed, even if I don't get the point of a lot of it and have significant disagreements with a lot of their stances, but I don't think I am doing that considering Petr Pavlensky's protest art resonated with me deeply. I was really inspired and understood the political powerlessness and malaise he felt, but what my untrained ass is articulating is that it primarily seems to appeal to other artistic types. What Pavlensky did was designed to be understood by the public, and he even explained the intent of his acts, but to most people he just publicly self-harmed. Before I became more familiar with theory, I always thought the appeal of performance art was the grotesqueness or spectacle of the act, but it usually isn't.

I probably won't start with anything that could hurt me or get me in legal trouble, because a clean legal bill is actually something I have to lose.
